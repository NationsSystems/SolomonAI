Knowledge Layer Integration

Amendment Number: I  
Adoption Date: 2026  
Applies To: The Solomon Constitution Version 1.1  
Status: Active

## Article I — Purpose of the Amendment

This amendment introduces a Knowledge Layer within the Investigator stage of the Solomon architecture.

The Knowledge Layer exists to provide deterministic classification signals derived from curated domain knowledge. These signals strengthen the evidentiary model used by the Orchestrator when determining execution routing.

The introduction of the Knowledge Layer does not alter the authority structure of the Solomon architecture and does not modify the deterministic orchestration model defined in the Constitution.

This amendment exists to formalize the architectural evolution identified during Phase 94 and implemented in Phase 95 of system development.

## Article II — Architectural Position of the Knowledge Layer

The Knowledge Layer operates exclusively within the Investigator stage of the Solomon execution pipeline.

The updated execution model becomes:

User Input  
↓  
Artifact Intake  
↓  
Investigator  
↓  
Knowledge Layer  
↓  
CaseFile  
↓  
Orchestrator  
↓  
Execution Lane  
↓  
Assistant Answer  
↓  
UX Synthesis Engine  
↓  
Packet Assembly  
↓  
User Interface

The Knowledge Layer is therefore an internal investigative mechanism that enriches the CaseFile with structured classification signals.

The Knowledge Layer does not exist as an independent architectural authority and does not modify the existing system authority chain.

## Article III — Function of the Knowledge Layer

The Knowledge Layer performs deterministic classification of artifacts and request properties using curated domain knowledge.

This may include detection of structured information properties such as:

• domain artifacts  
• document categories  
• dynamic information indicators  
• financial metrics  
• environmental conditions  
• industry-specific domain signals

These observations are emitted as structured signals and recorded within the CaseFile produced by the Investigator.

The purpose of these signals is to improve the reliability and explainability of routing decisions performed by the Orchestrator.

## Article IV — Deterministic Signal Emission

Signals generated by the Knowledge Layer must be deterministic.

Given identical input artifacts and request context, the Knowledge Layer must produce identical signals.

Signal emission must rely on explicit knowledge representations such as:

• curated keyword dictionaries  
• structured domain ontologies  
• deterministic classification rules

These signals are considered investigative observations and must be recorded within the CaseFile evidence structure.

## Article V — Authority Restrictions

The Knowledge Layer operates under the same witness-only authority model as the Investigator.

The Knowledge Layer may:

• analyze artifacts  
• classify domain signals  
• emit structured signals into the CaseFile

The Knowledge Layer must not:

• determine execution routing  
• select execution lanes  
• initiate system tasks  
• modify system execution flow

Execution authority remains exclusively with the Orchestrator.

## Article VI — Relationship to the Orchestrator

The Orchestrator remains the sole execution authority within the Solomon architecture.

Routing decisions must continue to be made by the Orchestrator using signals contained within the CaseFile.

Signals emitted by the Knowledge Layer serve as evidentiary inputs to the Orchestrator but do not determine execution behavior independently.

This preserves the Single Authority Principle defined in Article II of the Constitution.

## Article VII — Preservation of Architectural Integrity

The introduction of the Knowledge Layer does not modify:

• the authority chain of the Solomon architecture  
• the deterministic orchestration model  
• the role of the Investigator as an evidence witness  
• the authority of the Orchestrator  
• the responsibilities of execution lanes  
• the UX Synthesis Engine architecture

The Knowledge Layer functions strictly as an extension of the Investigator’s evidence observation capabilities.

## Article VIII — Compliance with System Integrity Rules

All implementations of the Knowledge Layer must comply with the System Integrity Rules defined in Article XI of the Solomon Constitution.

Specifically, implementations must not introduce:

• keyword-driven execution routing  
• routing logic outside the Orchestrator  
• probabilistic classification replacing deterministic signals  
• execution authority inside the Knowledge Layer

Violations of these constraints constitute architectural drift and must be corrected.

## Article IX — Relationship to Skill Lanes

The Knowledge Layer does not alter the Skill Lane architecture defined in Article VII of the Constitution.

Skill Lanes such as Bid Estimation Intelligence (BEI) continue to operate as execution modules invoked by the Orchestrator.

Signals emitted by the Knowledge Layer may assist the Orchestrator in determining when a Skill Lane should be invoked.

However, Skill Lane invocation remains exclusively under Orchestrator authority.

## Article X — Amendment Integration

This amendment becomes part of the Solomon Constitution upon adoption.

The Knowledge Layer is therefore recognized as a formal component of the Investigator stage of the Solomon architecture.

Future development must treat the Knowledge Layer as an investigative subsystem and must maintain the authority relationships defined in this amendment.

## Amendment Summary

This amendment formally integrates the Knowledge Layer into the Solomon architecture.

The Knowledge Layer improves routing reliability by introducing deterministic domain knowledge signals while preserving the constitutional principles of:

• deterministic orchestration  
• single execution authority  
• evidence-driven reasoning  
• modular execution lanes

The amendment therefore strengthens the evidentiary model used by the Orchestrator without altering the authority structure of the Solomon system.
